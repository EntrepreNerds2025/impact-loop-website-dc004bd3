# The Impact Loop Standard™
### A methodology for turning frontline impact into fundable, defensible data

*Methodology brief · Impact Loop · v1.0*

---

## Why this exists

Most community organizations can tell you they change lives. Very few can *show* it in a way a funder can trust. Attendance is easy to count — "heads in beds." Transformation is not. The growth a youth worker sees in a gym or a classroom lives in memory and scattered notes, and by grant-reporting season it has collapsed into a sentence: "we can see they've grown."

Funders increasingly won't accept that. They want outcomes, not activity. The Impact Loop Standard is a documented, repeatable way to convert what frontline staff observe every day into structured, longitudinal, auditable impact data — the kind that wins and renews funding.

It is deliberately built around one constraint: **it has to be effortless for the people doing the work.** A measurement system that adds an hour of paperwork gets abandoned by week three. Ours takes about fifteen seconds per observation.

---

## The core unit: the Pillar Moment

Everything in the Standard is built from one atomic record — the **Pillar Moment**. A staff member captures a single observation about a single participant:

> *who · which development pillar · how strong (1–5) · which growth indicator · direction (growth / neutral / setback) · a short note · timestamp*

Staff log it in three taps and a voice note. The note is the human story; the structured tags are what make it measurable. Thousands of these moments, accumulated over time, become an organization's impact dataset.

The Standard organizes growth into **five development pillars** — Identity, Character, Relationships, Service, and Purpose — each with named growth indicators. (The pillar set is configurable; an organization can adopt the default or map the Standard to its own outcomes framework.)

---

## The engine: how a moment becomes a number

Each pillar carries a score from 0–100, computed from the moments logged against it:

**PillarScore = (weighted average of the 1–5 ratings) × 20**

Two weights shape every moment's contribution:

- **Direction weight.** Growth moments count fully. Neutral observations count half. A *setback* keeps full weight but its rating drops to 1, so it actively pulls the score down. This is what keeps the Standard honest — it is not a feel-good meter.
- **Recency weight.** Moments decay on a 30-day half-life. An observation from today counts roughly twice one from a month ago, so a score reflects who the participant *is now*, not who they were a year ago.

**Growth Rate** is the headline funders care about: the change in a PillarScore versus the same score recomputed as of one quarter (91 days) earlier. "Service ▲ +16 points this quarter" is a real, reproducible number — and it expands, on demand, into the dated, named observations that produced it.

---

## What makes the data defensible

Three safeguards separate this from a vanity dashboard:

1. **Humans rate; the system only weights.** Staff assign every rating. The platform categorizes and aggregates — it never invents a score. Every number drills down to real, quotable observations a funder can audit.
2. **Confidence tiers.** A pillar's score is labeled *Emerging picture* (fewer than 3 observations), *Developing* (3–9), or *Strong evidence* (10+). One great day never masquerades as transformation.
3. **Self-evaluation cross-check.** Participants rate themselves each quarter on the same pillars. Where the participant's view and staff's view diverge, the gap becomes a coaching conversation — and a second, independent signal that the data is real.

---

## The output: numbers and stories, together

The Standard produces an **Impact Report** that pairs the quarter's growth figures with three anonymized observations in staff's own words — the evidence behind the metrics. Funders get the measurable outcome *and* the human moment that proves it, on one page, exportable on demand.

---

## Why this is an Impact Loop asset, not just a feature

The scoring formula is openly published — that is intentional. A formula is copyable; it is not the moat. What compounds and cannot be copied is built *around* it:

- **A standard.** A named, documented way to convert field observations into comparable outcomes — the equivalent of what accounting standards are to finance. When organizations and funders learn to speak in Pillar Moments and Growth Rates, Impact Loop owns the language.
- **Longitudinal benchmark data.** Years of dated observations across many organizations create norms no newcomer can replicate — "your Service pillar grew +19 this quarter; the network median is +12."
- **Funder trust.** As the methodology is validated and recognized, "measured on the Impact Loop Standard" becomes a credential a grant officer trusts on sight.
- **Workflow as system of record.** The daily logging habit makes Impact Loop the place an organization's impact history lives. Switching means abandoning the evidence behind its last three grant reports.

Give the formula away; own the standard, the data, the trust, and the workflow.

**The Impact Loop Standard turns fifteen-second frontline observations into longitudinal, auditable impact data — so the organizations doing the work can prove it, and the funders backing them can trust it.**
